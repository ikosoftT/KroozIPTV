## An EPG is a schedule, not the video itself
EPG stands for electronic program guide. It gives a player information about what is scheduled on a channel, such as program names and start and end times. Depending on the source and player, it may also include descriptions or categories. The player combines that schedule with its channel list to create a familiar television guide.

Guide information and video often travel through separate requests. A channel can play while its schedule is blank, and a complete-looking guide can remain visible when a stream is unavailable. This distinction matters when troubleshooting: a guide refresh problem is not automatically an account-login or internet-speed problem.

The amount of guide coverage depends on the supplied source and channel matching. Do not assume every channel has a detailed schedule or that every player receives the same number of future days. Ask support what is expected for the channels and package you use before treating a blank row as a device fault.

## How the guide is matched to channels
A player needs a way to associate a schedule entry with a channel in the playlist or account. That association can use identifiers supplied by the service. Display names alone are not always enough because channels can have similar names, regional versions, or different feeds with different schedules.

When identifiers do not match, video may work while the guide remains empty. Renaming a channel for your favorites list does not necessarily repair its underlying guide mapping. Some players offer manual matching, but use the app’s documented workflow and confirm the correct source rather than assigning a similar-looking name at random.

Guide formats vary, and some workflows use XMLTV data. The important user-level question is whether the player supports the source supplied with the account. Do not add an unrelated guide URL simply because it produces more rows; incorrect data can be more confusing than a clearly missing schedule.

## What to expect during the first import
An initial setup may load channels before guide data finishes downloading and processing. Allow the documented import process to complete. A large schedule can require storage and processing time, especially on an older TV. Repeatedly closing the app during import can make it harder to tell whether loading would have completed normally.

Check the player’s refresh status or last-updated information if it provides one. That can distinguish a guide that has not been downloaded from a guide that downloaded but did not match channels. Record any error message and the time. Avoid deleting a working video profile just because guide rows are not immediately visible.

Once the import completes, compare several channels you know should have guide coverage. A problem across the entire list suggests a different investigation from one missing regional feed. Use a current known program to check whether schedule times line up with actual viewing.

## Fix an incorrect time zone before adding offsets
Check the device’s date, time, and time zone first. A television set to a different region or a computer with an incorrect clock can display guide entries at the wrong time. Automatic time settings may help when they are correctly supported, but verify the actual displayed local time rather than assuming the setting is right.

Next, check whether the player has its own time-zone or guide-offset option. A manual offset left from an earlier setup can shift an otherwise correct schedule. Record the current setting before changing it. Applying a second correction on top of an existing one can make the guide look right briefly and wrong again after a clock change.

If only a specific channel is offset, the source may describe a different regional feed or use inconsistent timing. Do not shift the entire guide to correct one row. Report the channel, expected program, displayed program, and time zone so support can investigate the right mapping.

## Refresh guide data deliberately
Use the player’s normal guide-refresh control and wait for it to finish. Check that the device has internet access and enough storage. If the player offers automatic refresh intervals, use documented defaults or a reasonable supported setting rather than triggering constant updates in the hope of obtaining more content.

A refresh retrieves what the source currently provides; it cannot invent missing schedules or future programs beyond that source’s coverage. If a refresh succeeds but one channel stays blank, ask about coverage and mapping. If every refresh fails, investigate source access, account status, or the player’s compatibility with the guide format.

Clearing a guide cache can be useful in a documented troubleshooting sequence, but distinguish it from clearing all app data. The latter may erase playlists, credentials, favorites, and custom settings. Keep the working account configuration private and recorded before any action that removes stored data.

## When only one channel has no listings
Confirm the exact feed and region. A channel name can represent several versions with different schedules, and local affiliates may not share the same lineup. Compare other channels to verify that the guide source is generally loading. An isolated blank row does not justify resetting the entire network.

Ask support whether schedule data is available for that feed and whether its identifier should match automatically. If the player supports manual mapping, follow its documentation and use the confirmed identifier. Keep a note of the change so it can be reversed when the source is corrected.

Use the [channel inquiry page](/channels) if the more important question is whether the stream itself is available. Schedule data is not a guarantee of content rights or carriage. A listed event can be inaccurate, delayed, or associated with a different feed, so confirm time-sensitive viewing directly.

## Guide features are not playback permissions
A guide can show program names without offering replay, recording, or pause controls. Catch-up requires service support and appropriate content availability. Recording depends on the player, storage, permissions, and potentially an additional active connection. The presence of an icon or menu item does not establish those conditions.

If you want to record while watching another channel, ask how sessions are counted. A one-connection account may not support that combination even when the player’s interface offers it. Read the [multiple-device and connection guide](/blog/iptv-on-multiple-devices) before planning a household recording arrangement.

Parental controls are also separate from guide data. Program descriptions or age labels may be incomplete or absent. Use the player’s and device’s actual restriction features, test them, and do not rely on a guide category as the only safeguard for children’s viewing.

## Make a large guide easier to use
Start with a favorites list of the channels you actually watch. A smaller guide reduces navigation effort and makes missing or incorrect entries easier to notice. Choose clear local profile names and avoid creating multiple copies of the same playlist while experimenting with guide settings.

Adjust guide text size and layout if your player supports those controls. Test readability from the normal seating position rather than up close to the screen. Some TV interfaces are designed for different resolutions or remote behavior, so a player that looks attractive in a screenshot may not be comfortable in your room.

Keep custom offsets and mappings to a minimum unless they solve a confirmed issue. An account update or corrected source may make an old workaround unnecessary. When the guide changes unexpectedly, review your own adjustments before assuming the service is sending incorrect data.

## A guide-specific support checklist
Report whether video plays, whether all channels or one channel lacks listings, the last successful refresh, the device time zone, and any manual offset. Include the player version and the affected feed’s exact name. A screenshot can help if account details and private URLs are hidden first.

Explain what you expected to see and when. “The guide is wrong” is less useful than “This channel shows a program two hours earlier than local time while the others are correct.” That detail helps separate clock settings from a regional-feed mismatch or source-data issue.

For a complete playback failure, use the [main troubleshooting guide](/blog/iptv-not-working) instead of only refreshing schedules. During a [trial](/free-trial), test guide usability alongside picture and sound before choosing a [subscription duration](/pricing). The guide is part of the viewing experience, but it should be evaluated as its own component.
